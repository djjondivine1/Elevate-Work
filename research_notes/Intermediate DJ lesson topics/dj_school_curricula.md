# How DJ schools and online courses structure INTERMEDIATE lessons

Method and limits (read first): the sandbox egress policy blocked direct page fetches (HTTP 403 on CONNECT for digitaldjtips.com, dj.studio, wearecrossfader.co.uk, pointblankmusicschool.com, dubspot and others). All provider data below comes from WebSearch result summaries of the providers' own pages, not from reading the full pages. Consequences: (a) several syllabi are partial, (b) "exact lesson titles" are exact only where marked with quotes or listed as titles in the summary, (c) frequency counts are lower bounds based on what surfaced. Where a full lesson list sits behind a paywall or was not retrievable, it is listed under Gaps. The technique steps in Key Question 4 are NOT from a single cited curriculum page. They are my synthesis of standard controller/CDJ practice, shown under Inferences so the report writer can treat them as unsourced-but-standard and fact-check before publishing.

Already-covered topics (overlap noted only): waveform shapes, quick-cut transition, midrange vocal separation, resonance control, EQ + filter combo, old-song-to-remix, DJ download stores.

## Q1. Which DJ schools / academies / course providers publish curricula or lesson lists? (target: 8+)

### Takeaway
At least 19 distinct providers publish some level-2 / intermediate curriculum detail: 13 with usable topic lists (Scratch DJ Academy, Point Blank, Dubspot, Crossfader, Digital DJ Tips, Sound Collective, Jak Bradley on Skillshare/Udemy, Learning to DJ, London Sound Academy, Make Me a DJ, Technique Music Academy, On Tempo DJ, Toolroom Academy), plus DJ Courses Online, WDC DJ Academy, Berklee Online, DJ.Studio, Algoriddim and AlphaTheta with thinner or non-intermediate detail. Official manufacturer hubs (AlphaTheta, Serato) did not surface an intermediate tier.

### Inferences
- Manufacturer hubs mostly teach "how to use our gear/software" rather than leveled technique curricula, so schools and course sellers are the better source of "what is intermediate".
- Eight of the 13 detailed providers are Rekordbox/CDJ-centred (Crossfader, LSA, Make Me a DJ, On Tempo, Toolroom, Technique, Scratch L2 lists both Serato and Rekordbox, AlphaTheta), reflecting current club-standard gear.

### Gaps
- Dubspot's current official Level 2 page, Pioneer DJ/AlphaTheta Academy intermediate lessons, Serato's own tutorial hub, Skilz DJ Academy module list, and Digital DJ Tips' own syllabus pages could not be read (blocked or not surfaced).
- No local-school pages beyond UK/NYC/Barcelona were checked.

## Q2. What does each provider call "intermediate" and what are the lesson titles / topics at that level?

### Takeaway
Everyone uses the same pivot: beginner = beatmatch + EQ blend; intermediate = prep tools (cues, loops, grids, library), harmonic mixing, effects, phrase-aware transitions, and a first recorded or performed set. Titles below are exact where in quotes; otherwise they are topic lists from the provider's description.

### Cited Findings

**1. Scratch DJ Academy - "Level 2: Intermediate Mixing"** ([source](https://academy.scratch.com/courses/dj-certification))
- Manual beatmatching and phrasing; Serato and Rekordbox software; cue points and loops; key mixing with the Camelot system; creating playlists/crates and file organization; ends with a ~10-minute performed set.
- Level 3 "Intermediate Scratching": chirp, stab, transform; timing and record control; using tricks inside live sets.
- Stand-alone "Intermediate Mixing (M 202)": 12 sessions, mixing across genres and transition timing, 10-minute final set ([listing](https://coursehorse.com/nyc/classes?school=scratch-dj-academy-nyc)). Sources disagree on program length (6 months twice weekly vs ~1 year weekly).

**2. Point Blank - "DJ Skills Level 2 / Creative DJ Skills" (10 weeks)** ([DJ Mag](https://djmag.com/news/point-blank-launches-new-beginner-and-advanced-dj-skills-courses), [online page](https://www.pointblankmusicschool.com/courses/online/short/dj-skills-level-2/))
- Harmonic mixing, acapella mixing, scratching, audio effects, recording/editing mixes, making DJ drops, building a sonic identity/brand. Online = weekly 2-hour live session (recorded).
- Week-by-week titles not retrievable.

**3. Dubspot - Digital DJing with Traktor, "Level 2"** ([source](https://blog.dubspot.com/digital-djing-traktor-program))
- Song structure, counting bars, moving from beat-aligned to phrase-aligned mixing. Undated post; treat as legacy.

**4. Crossfader - "Rekordbox Intermediate DJ Course" (41 lessons per course page; some listings say 49 lessons / 7.5 h)** ([source](https://app.wearecrossfader.co.uk/online-dj-courses/rekordbox-intermediate))
- Hot cues, loops, FX, key mixing, track separation (stems). Older blog outline: Part 1 performance pads, Part 2 mixing different styles of music. Free sample lessons: "Pitch Effect Tricks" ([link](https://wearecrossfader.co.uk/blog/pitch-effect-tricks/)); "Create a HUGE drop using Serato DJ Pro FX" (version echo effect, from their intermediate course) ([link](https://wearecrossfader.co.uk/blog/create-a-huge-drop-in-serato-dj-pro/)); open-format page previews bridging 140 to 172 BPM with loops/sync and a hot-cue sample switch-up on a DDJ-FLX4 ([link](https://wearecrossfader.co.uk/blog/open-format-djing/)).

**5. Digital DJ Tips courses** (not labelled "level 2"; positioned beyond beginner)
- James Hype's Mixing Skills: 9 modules, 48 lessons, 53 demos. Module 4 "Hype Loops" (loop-based tension build before a drop), Module 5 combining techniques into full transitions, Module 6 complex arrangements/extra decks/restructuring and mashups, Module 7 signature routines, Module 8 small/medium controllers. Topic list includes loops, hot cues, keymixing, quantize, filters, spinbacks, pitch and tempo, acapellas, echo, four-deck mixing ([course page](https://www.digitaldjtips.com/dj-courses/james-hypes-mixing-skills/)).
- House Mixing Mastery: 48 mix/transition techniques incl. loops, hot cues, FX and filters, acapellas, keymixing, genre/BPM changes; "The 7 Rules of House Music" (when/where to mix, matching elements, key sync, tension and release); bonus modules on manual beatmixing and beatgridding ([page](https://pages.digitaldjtips.com/mps-evergreen-new-template1595413623524)).
- Ferry Corsten trance course: 62 lessons / 8 modules incl. phrase mixing, volume control, bass/mid/high EQ ([info](https://www.digitaldjtips.com/new-ferry-corsten-course-info/)).
- Acapella Mixing Masterclass: keymixing, loop tricks, finding acapellas, live remix/mashup ([page](https://www.digitaldjtips.com/dj-courses/acapella-mixing-masterclass/)).

**6. Sound Collective**
- "DJ Mixing Techniques and Phrasing" (1 h 44 m, Proper Villains) — sections and lessons: Song structure and setup (pop/hip-hop structures, dance-music structures, setting up hot cues, gridding incl. live music and tempo-transition tools, harmonic mixing); Transitions (standard, echo, beat-mixing for hip-hop, intro pick-up, wordplay); Creative blends (blending styles with harmonic mixing, mashup, mixing dance songs, longer mixing); Advanced moves (highlighting sections of a mix, phrase cheating with loops, double drops, riddim chopping) ([course](https://soundcollective.com/courses/dj-mixing-techniques-and-phrasing/)).
- "Intermediate Serato" (12 h, $950): song structure and phrasing, transitions, live looping, stems, FX, gig prep, etiquette, promotion; prerequisite Intro to Serato ([page](https://soundcollective.com/stp/intermediate-serato/?v=0b3b97fa6688)).

**7. Jak Bradley, Skillshare "Complete DJ Course for Beginners" - Intermediate section** ([Skillshare](https://www.skillshare.com/en/classes/the-complete-dj-course-for-beginners-a-full-step-by-step-guide-to-djing/1692694063))
- Intro to intermediate mixing; Equalizers and Filters; ten guided intermediate mixes; "Mixing In Key" (placed between mixes 8 and 9); sampler lesson. Hot cues and loops sit in his Advanced section. Udemy version has a different list (intermediate section ~15 lectures, 1 h 49 m) ([Udemy](https://www.udemy.com/course/10steps2dj/)).

**8. Learning to DJ - Beginner/Intermediate House & Techno** (8 sections, 139 lectures, ~10.5 h)
- Beatmatching, EQ, song structure, phrasing, key and tempo; blends, cuts, overlays; Open Key harmonic mixing; track discovery; online presence; recording/mastering sets. Spanish listing names modules: introduction, fundamental skills, mixing techniques, advanced DJ topics, music discovery and flow, growing as a DJ, reflecting ([EN](https://www.udemy.com/course/l2dj-btoi/), [ES](https://www.udemy.com/course/l2dj-btoi-es/)). Separate "Mistake Recovery for House/Techno DJs" course ([post](https://learningtodj.com/blog/l2dj-practicals-mistake-recovery/)).

**9. London Sound Academy - Intermediate DJ Course (4 x 2 h)** ([source](https://www.londonsoundacademy.com/dj-courses/intermediate-dj-course))
- Module "Creative Settings on Rekordbox": setting hot cues, setting loops, organising metadata, build a playlist for creative mixing; advanced beatgridding; A-Z of Pioneer CDJs and mixers; Beat FX, vinyl mode, acapella, cutting/genre-specific techniques (from related page).

**10. Make Me a DJ - Intermediate DJ Course (10 single-video sections)** ([source](https://makemeadj.com/online-courses/intermediate-dj-course/))
- Intro; setting up Rekordbox; beat grids and cue points; planning a set and exporting to USB; CDJ-3000 and pitch control; loops; EQ and filter mixing; basic effects on DJM-A9; full walkthrough; recording a set.

**11. Technique Music Academy - DJ Intermediate** ([source](https://www.techniquemusicacademy.co.uk/course/dj-intermediate/))
- Consolidating mixing (fixing problems, EQ, monitoring, headphone levels, level matching, phrasing); CD mixing (vinyl mode, pitch, cue points, loops, hot cues for samples); MP3 mixing with Serato; effects and sampling (delay, flange, phasing, filter, sampler); recording and editing a mix. Dated syllabus (CDJ-1000/DJM-800 era).

**12. On Tempo DJ - Level 2** (6 x 90 min) ([source](https://ontempodj.com/intermediate-course/))
- EQ-based blending; mixer main effects for build/tension/release; filters; cutting/stabbing with up-faders and crossfader; Rekordbox set prep (saved cue/loop points on USB); CDJ looping, hot cues, syncing; DVS, controllers, recording and sharing mixes.

**13. Toolroom Academy DJ Masterclass (Devstar)** ([Thinkific](https://toolroomacademy.thinkific.com/courses/DJMasterclass), [review](https://djtechtools.com/2020/08/26/review-toolroom-academys-online-dj-masterclass/))
- M1 setup, beatmatch, phrase mixing; M2 EQ and ear beatmatching; M3 alternative transitions (exiting a busy mix, reverse tracks, CDJ looping); M4 creative looping and basic mixer effects (echo, delay, reverb, filters); M5 organising music in Rekordbox and mixing in key; later modules reportedly 3-deck mixing and advanced Rekordbox (not confirmed). Lesson count: 85 (Toolroom) vs 88 (review).

**14. DJ Courses Online** — DJ Techniques II: intro to cue points, setting cues in Traktor/Serato, MIDI mapping, looping ([II](https://courses.djcoursesonline.com/p/dj-techniques-ii)). DJ Techniques III: sampling a clip into a deck, recording a mix, backspinning and looping in Serato DJ ([III](https://courses.djcoursesonline.com/p/dj-techniques-iii)). Software generation is older (Scratch Live).

**15. WDC DJ Academy - Intermediate (17-18 lessons)** ([source](https://www.wdcdjacademy.co.uk/intermediate-dj-course/))
- Presentation and gear, library management, planning a mix, counting beats/bars/phrases, mixing in key, "quick mix" skill, tempo changes, effects, staying ahead of the room, reading the crowd, backup planning. Per-lesson titles not retrieved.

**16. Berklee Online - Learn to DJ with Traktor** ([source](https://online.berklee.edu/courses/learn-to-dj-with-traktor))
- Beatmatching, dropping on the one, EQing, cue points, looping, beatgridding, sync (tempo and phase, master clock; sync treated as debated), BPM blending, a "halfway method" exercise. Page metadata is about 9 years old.

**17. DJ.Studio** — intermediate framed as moving from single transitions to coherent 5-10 track sets with a deliberate energy shape; expansion topics: longer blends, tempo changes, key changes, breakdown transitions, loop-based transitions; workflow = Harmonize for order, then edit transitions and fix beatgrids ([course map](https://dj.studio/blog/theory-practice-dj-course-map), [flexible training](https://dj.studio/blog/flexible-dj-training-every-level)). Key-compatibility drills via Camelot game ([Camelot guide](https://dj.studio/blog/camelot-wheel)). Vendor-side description.

**18. Algoriddim djay** — per-stem volume faders; waveform view of up to four stems (drums, bass, harmonics/vocals); routing FX to individual stems; Neural Mix disabled for streaming-service tracks ([tracks](https://help.algoriddim.com/user-manual/djay-pro-mac/neural-mix/tracks), [FX](https://help.algoriddim.com/user-manual/djay-pro-mac/neural-mix/fx)); controller pad mapping for stems ([Crossfader](https://wearecrossfader.co.uk/blog/midi-mapping-algoriddim-djay-neural-mix/)).

**19. AlphaTheta x FEMME HOUSE (intro only)** — rekordbox prep, song structure, beatmatching, cue points, transitions, effects, energy control ([Rekkerd](https://rekkerd.org/alphatheta-and-femme-house-launch-online-intro-to-djing-course-for-aspiring-djs/)). Shows cue points/effects/energy are creeping into the "intro" tier.

### Inferences
- Labels used: "Level 2" (Scratch, Point Blank, On Tempo, Dubspot), "Intermediate" (Crossfader, Sound Collective, LSA, Make Me a DJ, Technique, WDC, Skillshare section), "Creative DJ Skills" (Point Blank), "Techniques II/III" (DJ Courses Online), "Mixing Mastery/Skills" (Digital DJ Tips). No provider uses "improver" or "beyond the basics" as a formal level name in what I found.
- Typical intermediate runtime is small: 4x2 h to 12 sessions; online video 1-10 h.

### Gaps
- Full lesson-by-lesson titles for Crossfader (paywalled), Point Blank (week-by-week), Dubspot, Toolroom M6+, WDC, Digital DJ Tips modules 1-3 of James Hype were not retrievable.
- Skilz DJ Academy (28 h one-to-one beginner-to-intermediate) not examined in detail ([listing](https://www.skilzdjacademy.com/courses/prime-dj-course-[intensive-individual-course])).

### Inferences (synthesis: unsourced standard practice, verify before publishing)

1. **Looping.** What: repeat a bar-quantized slice of audio. Steps: enable quantize; set auto-loop length (4/8 beats, or 1/2/1/4 for rolls); trigger loop on the downbeat; play the incoming track under it; release the loop on the phrase boundary; optionally halve loop length each bar to build tension. Why: extends an intro/outro so you never run out of track, builds drops. Mistakes: looping off-grid (loop catches a transient early), leaving the loop on past the phrase, loop on a vocal that cuts mid-word, forgetting loop clears when track changes. Drill: loop the last 4 bars of a track, bring in the next track, release exactly on bar 1 of the next phrase, 10 times.
2. **Hot cues.** What: stored jump points. Steps: grid first; drop cue on bar 1 of intro, first drop, breakdown, outro; colour-code; use pads to jump on the beat. Why: instant access to phrases, rescue a mistimed transition, sample switch-ups. Mistakes: setting cues before beatgrid is correct, too many unlabeled cues, jumping off-beat without quantize. Drill: set 4 cues (intro/break/drop/outro) on five tracks and mix between them using only cue jumps.
3. **Harmonic mixing.** What: pick tracks whose keys are compatible. Steps: analyze library for keys; use Camelot or Open Key; safe moves = same code, +/-1 number same letter, A to B switch same number; keep pitch/tempo changes small (key shifts with tempo unless key lock); blend over breakdowns/bass-light sections. Why: smooth melodic blends without clash. Mistakes: trusting auto-analysis blindly, ignoring key drift from big pitch moves, long blends when basslines clash anyway. Drill: build an 8-track chain moving +1 around the wheel and note how each blend feels.
4. **Effects (echo, reverb, filter FX).** What: time-based FX for transitions and tension. Steps: choose FX, set beat-synced time, ramp wet/dry rather than slam, cut the dry track as echo tail rings, switch off on the downbeat of the new phrase. Why: covers rough joins, builds energy. Mistakes: leaving FX on too long, muddy low end from reverb on bass, hitting FX off the grid. Drill: echo-out three tracks in a row with a clean cut into the next phrase.
5. **Phrasing/song structure.** What: tracks are built in 8/16/32-bar blocks. Steps: count 1-8 per bar group; identify intro, build, drop, breakdown, outro; start the incoming track so its drop lands on the outgoing breakdown/outro boundary; change at phrase starts. Why: transitions feel intentional. Mistakes: mixing in mid-phrase, miscounting after tempo-free sections, only watching waveforms. Drill: count aloud and mark every 16-bar boundary on one track by ear.
6. **Library and set prep.** What: structure so gigs are repeatable. Steps: clean tags/genres; analyze (grids, keys); colour and rate; build playlists by energy/role; set hot cues; export to USB; run a test load. Why: fewer on-stage surprises. Mistakes: analyzing on stage, mismatched export formats, no backup copy. Drill: prep a 45-minute playlist and boot it on a second device.
7. **Acapellas/stems/mashups.** What: layer isolated parts. Steps: pick same/compatible key and tempo; load acapella or stem-separated vocal on deck B; mute the outgoing vocal, keep drums/bass; fade in the new part at a phrase start; leave space. Why: creates new moments from existing tracks. Mistakes: two vocals at once, key clash, using low-quality stem separation artifacts. Drill: layer one acapella over three different instrumentals.
8. **Transition variety.** What: more tools than a long blend. Steps: learn echo-out, spinback/backspin, cut on the drop, double drop (match two drops by phrase), reverse. Why: matches transitions to genre and energy. Mistakes: reaching for gimmicks every mix, double drops with clashing keys. Drill: do each transition once on the same pair of tracks and rate them.
9. **Beatgridding.** What: align grid to the transients. Steps: set first downbeat marker; check grid at start, middle, end; adjust for variable-tempo (live) tracks; fix before cues. Why: sync, loops and quantize depend on it. Mistakes: trusting auto-grid on drifting tracks, grid on a pickup beat. Drill: correct five auto-gridded tracks and check by looping at the end.
10. **Genre/BPM changes.** What: move between tempos. Steps: use a bridge track, loop-and-ramp, half/double-time (e.g. 140 to 70 feel), or an echo/breakdown cut; keep pitch ranges small. Why: open-format sets. Mistakes: huge pitch bends, no musical bridge. Drill: go from 124 to 140 BPM in 32 bars with a loop.
11. **Recording a mix.** What: capture a clean take. Steps: set gain in the green, master peaking below clipping, limit FX; record one pass; trim and label. Why: portfolio and feedback. Mistakes: red-lining master, restarting on small errors. Drill: record a 15-minute mix and review weak transitions.
12. **Set building/energy.** What: arc of a set. Steps: map warm-up, build, peak, release; order tracks; read the room. Why: keeps the floor. Mistakes: peaking too early, all tracks same energy. Drill: sketch an energy curve for a 5-track mini-set and play it.
13. **Manual beatmatch vs sync.** What: match tempo by ear; use sync as a tool. Steps: match BPM, then nudge/pitch-ride for phase; use sync for loops/FX; know master tempo and key lock. Why: recover when sync fails. Mistakes: relying on sync with bad grids. Drill: beatmatch 3 pairs by ear with sync off.
14. **Scratch/sampler basics.** What: baby scratch, chirp, stab, transform; sampler hits. Steps: slow baby scratch, cut with the crossfader, one-shot samples on the beat. Why: expressive layer. Mistakes: scratching off-beat, too much. Drill: 8 bars of baby scratch in time to a 90 BPM track.
15. **Multi-deck layering.** What: use 3-4 decks. Steps: drums on deck A, loop acapella on deck C, bring in a new bass; EQ lanes. Why: depth. Mistakes: every deck full range, mud. Drill: layer a loop under a two-track blend with strict EQ separation.
