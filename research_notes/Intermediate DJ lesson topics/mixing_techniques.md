## 1. Phrasing and 8/16/32-bar structure ("mixing on the phrase")

### Takeaway
Dance music is built in blocks of 4, 8, 16 and 32 bars; the biggest changes (drops, breakdowns, new elements) land on those boundaries, so a transition sounds intentional only if the incoming track's first bar (the "1") starts on the outgoing track's phrase boundary. Count bars, not just beats, and use beat jump or phrase counters to stay oriented.

### Cited Findings
- Common DJ phrase lengths are 4, 8, 16 and 32 bars, with most changes landing every 8 or 16 bars. A phrase is a distinct section (intro, verse, chorus, breakdown). — [dj.studio: phrasing in DJ mixing](https://dj.studio/blog/phrasing-dj-mixing) (via search summary)
- Forum DJs count in 32 beats because 8 bars (32 beats) is how long a complete musical idea typically takes to state; a Traktor-oriented guide notes audible changes typically occur every 32 beats. — [DJ TechTools forum: tips on phrase matching](https://forum.djtechtools.com/t/tips-on-phrase-matching/49511) and [forum thread: phrase mixing question](https://forum.djtechtools.com/t/really-quick-question-about-phrase-mixing/34019) (forum, via search summary)
- One forum poster argues for starting the incoming track on the 16th bar rather than the 8th because breaks and drops are multiples of 16, not necessarily 8. — [DJ TechTools forum: mixing on the fly](https://forum.djtechtools.com/t/question-about-mixing-on-the-fly/41838) (forum)
- Worked alignment example from a forum: with a 32-bar outro and an incoming track that has an 8-bar intro, start the incoming track 24 bars into the outgoing track's outro so both phrase boundaries (the incoming drop and the outgoing outro end) line up. — [DJ TechTools forum: mixing on the fly](https://forum.djtechtools.com/t/question-about-mixing-on-the-fly/41838) (forum)
- Traktor: setting "Bars Per Phrase" to 8 makes the deck header display phrase, bar and beat counts so you can see when the next phrase starts; beat-jumping by 32 beats (one 8-bar phrase) helps keep your place. — [DJ TechTools forum: tips on phrase matching](https://forum.djtechtools.com/t/tips-on-phrase-matching/49511) (forum)
- Caveat: real tracks often have an extra bar or uneven intro/outro that breaks the count, so verify by ear. — [DJ TechTools forum: phrase matching and musical structure](https://forum.djtechtools.com/t/question-about-phrase-matching-mixing-to-compliment-a-tracks-musical-structure/68140) (forum)
- Coming in mid-phrase makes even a perfect beatmatch sound off; start the incoming track on a phrase boundary. — [Digital DJ Pool: DJ transitions](https://digitaldjpool.com/blog/dj-transitions-for-beginners/) (via search summary)
- Mixgraph also lists a 2026 "make better mixes" guide on phrasing. — [DJworx: How to Make Better DJ Mixes in 2026](https://djworx.com/?p=46933) (appeared in results; contents not summarized, so not used for claims)

### Inferences
- Step recipe for a controller (standard practice, unsourced): (1) Load incoming track on the cue deck and set a hot cue/memory cue on beat 1 of its first phrase (or the drop). (2) Listen to the outgoing track and find where its outro/breakdown phrase begins; count "1 2 3 4 / 2 2 3 4 ... 8 2 3 4" per bar. (3) Press play on the incoming track exactly on the downbeat of the outgoing phrase's first bar (hot cue launch, or hold cue and release). (4) Beatmatch/sync, then bring the fader/EQ up over 8-16 bars so the swap happens when the phrases end together.
- Reasoning to teach: because arrangement elements enter every 8/16/32 bars, aligning phrases means the new track's kick/bass/vocal arrives exactly when the old one's fades, instead of two arrangements fighting.
- Typical mistakes: counting beats without knowing where "1" of the phrase is; trusting an auto beatgrid whose downbeat is on the wrong beat; starting the blend 4 bars early or late so drops collide; over-relying on waveform instead of ears (the waveform lesson is already published, so only mention).
- Practice drill: pick two house tracks at similar BPM. Without any transition, just count a full 32 bars on one deck out loud and note what changes at bars 8, 16, 32. Then launch track B from its first-phrase hot cue on bar 1 of track A's last 32-bar section and do a 16-bar blend. Repeat with track B's drop landing on A's phrase end.

### Gaps
- No official AlphaTheta/Pioneer, Serato or rekordbox page was readable for their phrase displays (rekordbox has phrase analysis; Serato has no native phrase counter, but this is unverified here). Need manual check.
- No source for a standardized "how many bars are in a typical intro/outro" figure beyond forum claims.

## 2. Harmonic mixing (Camelot wheel / Open Key, energy boost, key lock)

### Takeaway
The Camelot wheel labels 24 keys 1A-12B (A = minor, B = major); safest mixes are same code, +1, -1, or same number with the A/B letter swapped. "+2" is a deliberate energy boost that shares fewer notes, so time it across drums/no melodies. Harmonic compatibility does not guarantee a good mix (energy, vocals, tempo still matter), and key lock is optional.

### Cited Findings
- Camelot wheel maps 24 keys onto a numbered circle; A = minor, B = major; neighbors on the wheel are harmonically compatible. — [Mixed In Key: how to use the Camelot wheel](https://mixedinkey.com/workflows/how-to-use-the-camelot-wheel/) (via search summary)
- Safest moves: same code, one number up, one number down, or the matching A/B position (e.g., from 8A: 7A, 8A, 9A, 8B). — [Mixed In Key: Camelot wheel](https://mixedinkey.com/workflows/how-to-use-the-camelot-wheel/); [dj.studio: compatible keys](https://dj.studio/blog/compatible-keys)
- +2 "energy boost" (e.g., 8A to 10A) is the stronger lift; musically it is one whole step up (A minor to B minor), and the keys share fewer notes, so don't make the jump while two melodies overlap; suggested fix is to time the jump across a percussive section so the key change lands as the new melody arrives. — [Mixed In Key: change energy with the Camelot wheel](https://mixedinkey.com/workflows/change-energy-with-camelot-wheel/); [Digital DJ Pool: harmonic mixing](https://digitaldjpool.com/blog/harmonic-mixing-camelot-wheel/)
- +7 jump (e.g., 8A to 3A) is one semitone up and described as aggressive. — [Digital DJ Pool: harmonic mixing](https://digitaldjpool.com/blog/harmonic-mixing-camelot-wheel/)
- A real clash example: mixing 5A into 10A creates uncomfortable dissonance. — [Mixed In Key: how to use the Camelot wheel](https://mixedinkey.com/workflows/how-to-use-the-camelot-wheel/)
- If compatible keys still sound off, likely causes: wrong key detection (check by ear), competing vocals/melodies, big tempo gap, or energy mismatch. Key detection is helpful, not perfect. — [Mixed In Key](https://mixedinkey.com/?p=324) / [Mixgraph key compatibility tool](https://www.mixgraph.io/tools/key-compatibility) (via search summary)
- Mixed In Key recommends pairing key with an energy level, since a compatible key does not guarantee the right energy. — [Mixed In Key: energy](https://mixedinkey.com/workflows/change-energy-with-camelot-wheel/)
- Some DJs argue for intuition over strict key rules; Mixed In Key itself says the wheel does not replace listening. — [MusicianWave: Camelot wheel](https://www.musicianwave.com/camelot-wheel-mixing-in-key/) (via search summary)
- Key lock/master tempo: keeps pitch constant when tempo changes; not mandatory. It gives cleaner transitions when mixing in key and avoids odd pitch shifts from jog-wheel nudges. — [We Are Crossfader: DJ tips, key lock & master tempo](https://wearecrossfader.co.uk/blog/key-lock/)
- When NOT to rely on it / limits: audio quality degrades for tempo changes beyond roughly 8% and software struggles to hold the key; with a DVS+CDJ setup keep master tempo off and use software key lock; VirtualDJ's "pure scratch" master tempo mode disables keylock; for small tempo deviations you can skip time-stretching and compute keys at a normalized tempo instead. — [We Are Crossfader: key lock](https://wearecrossfader.co.uk/blog/key-lock/); [VirtualDJ forum: key lock no longer functions](https://virtualdj.com/forums/168026/Old_versions/Key-lock_no-longer_functions..html); [DJ TechTools forum: tempo based key normalisation](https://forum.djtechtools.com/t/tempo-based-key-normalisation/65805)
- Anecdotal (forum): Traktor's keylock is fine for slight shifts but degrades audio when the tempo knob is moved actively. — [DJ TechTools forum: Traktor equivalent for CDJ Master Tempo](https://forum.djtechtools.com/t/traktor-equivalent-for-cdj-master-tempo/24627)

### Inferences
- Open Key notation (used by Traktor) is a relabeling of the same wheel; I did not retrieve a sourced conversion table. Note for carousel: say "Camelot (rekordbox/Mixed In Key) vs Open Key (Traktor); same idea, different numbers" and verify the mapping before printing numbers.
- Steps (unsourced standard practice): analyze library in the software; show key column; pick next track within +/-1 or A/B swap; keep harmonic changes during drum-only sections; with key lock on, tempo changes won't alter the key shown; if you push a tempo past ~6-8% with key lock off, the key shifts (each ~6% is about a semitone), so a "compatible" key can become a clash.
- Mistakes: trusting auto key detection blindly; blending a long overlap with two vocals; using +2 or +7 over overlapped melodies; assuming same key means same energy.
- Drill: take one track in 8A and build a 5-track chain moving only 8A -> 9A -> 9B -> 8B -> 10B style, noting which moves felt like lifts. Then do one deliberate +2 jump with the melody dropped out (drums only for 8 bars).

### Gaps
- Could not read Open Key vs Camelot conversion or the Traktor/rekordbox key display specifics. No data on whether Serato/rekordbox/VDJ key lock algorithms differ in quality.
- The "6% = 1 semitone" fact is general music knowledge; I did not source it.

## 3. Long blends vs short blends

### Takeaway
Long blends (16-32+ bars) suit tracks with compatible keys, similar energy, and sparse/non-vocal overlap zones; short blends or cuts suit rhythmically varied genres (hip-hop, grime), vocal-heavy or clashing sections, and sudden energy changes. Choose the length the pair can survive.

### Cited Findings
- Long blends (16-32 bars or more) suit smooth, hypnotic sets, associated with house and techno; they work best with high harmonic compatibility (same/adjacent Camelot key) and similar energy; sparse intros/outros overlap happily. — [Mixgraph: DJ transition techniques](https://www.mixgraph.io/learn/dj-transition-techniques); [Digital DJ Pool: DJ transitions](https://digitaldjpool.com/blog/dj-transitions-for-beginners/)
- Short cuts/swaps fit clashing tracks or when the energy needs a jolt; two dense, vocal-heavy sections clash, so cut on a downbeat. Four-to-the-floor genres blend long; hip-hop/grime suit quick cuts. — [Mixgraph: DJ transition techniques](https://www.mixgraph.io/learn/dj-transition-techniques); [Violet Recording: beat mixing vs blending](https://violetrecording.com/beat-mixing-vs-blending/)
- Warning: a 32-bar blend of two competing vocal hooks sounds chaotic regardless of beatmatch; shorten overlap or change technique. — [Mixgraph: DJ transition techniques](https://www.mixgraph.io/learn/dj-transition-techniques)
- Middle ground: echo-out or filter-out transitions. — [Mixgraph: DJ transition techniques](https://www.mixgraph.io/learn/dj-transition-techniques)
- Quick cut transition is already published: only one-line mention in carousel.
- Forum thread on long vs short mixes by region (opinion only, not summarized): [DJ TechTools forum: long or short mixes](https://forum.djtechtools.com/t/long-or-short-mixes-in-your-area/38642)

### Inferences
- Decision checklist for carousel: Same genre/dense groove + compatible keys + both intros sparse = long blend. Vocals on top of each other, different genres, or room energy needs a spike = short blend/cut/echo-out.
- Practice drill: mix the same two tracks three ways (32-bar blend, 8-bar blend, 1-bar cut on downbeat) and record which one suits; note what each does to the crowd.

### Gaps
- No quantitative data on crowd response; mostly opinion. Conflict noted: sources disagree on whether genre or key matters more.

## 4. Bass swap and 3-band EQ transition recipes

### Takeaway
The bass swap is the core long-blend move: bring the incoming track in with its low EQ cut, then swap the two lows on a phrase change, so only one bassline plays at a time. The goal is replacement, not adding weight.

### Cited Findings
- Recipe: match tempo and phrase; turn the incoming track's low EQ down before opening its fader; bring it in over 8 or 16 bars; at the next phrase, swap the low EQ controls; fade out track A while B's full groove takes over. — [Digital DJ Pool: DJ EQ mixing](https://digitaldjpool.com/blog/dj-eq-mixing-for-beginners/); [Digital DJ Pool: transitions](https://digitaldjpool.com/blog/dj-transitions-for-beginners/)
- Alternate recipe: 16-32 bar overlap, gradually increasing incoming volume while decreasing outgoing, use EQ to carve space, swap bass in one move at the switch point. — [dj.studio: mix two songs](https://dj.studio/blog/mix-two-songs)
- Mistakes: boosting both bass controls (two basslines = muddy); lowering the gain after cutting bass (channel seems quieter; set gain while EQ is flat); starting the incoming track mid-phrase. — [Mixgraph: bass swap glossary](https://www.mixgraph.io/glossary/bass-swap); [Digital DJ Pool: EQ](https://digitaldjpool.com/blog/dj-eq-mixing-for-beginners/)
- Slow EQ blending suited to techno is covered here: [We Are Crossfader: slow EQ blends](https://wearecrossfader.co.uk/?p=223713) (appeared in results; not summarized).
- EQ+filter combo is already published; mention in one line only.

### Inferences
- Controller steps (unsourced standard practice): channel gain set with EQ flat -> incoming low knob fully left, mids/highs at noon -> fader up gradually (or up fully, using EQ as the blend) -> on phrase boundary, bring incoming low to noon while taking outgoing low to zero in one smooth/quick motion -> over next 8 bars bring outgoing mids/highs out and fader down. Variant: swap mids last for a "vocal handoff" (midrange separation lesson already published; skip details).
- Practice drill: take two bassy tracks and do a bass swap five times at 16 bars; record, listen for low-end volume dip or build-up at the swap moment.

### Gaps
- Exact kill/cut vs "fully down" behavior differs by mixer/controller (some EQs are isolators); no source retrieved.

## 5. Filter sweeps and echo-outs

### Takeaway
Filter sweeps use a high-pass to build tension or thin out the outgoing track and a low-pass to dim/exit it; echo-outs let a delay tail carry the outgoing track away after you drop its fader at the end of a phrase.

### Cited Findings
- High-pass removes bass (thinner, builds tension); low-pass removes highs (darker, good for fading out). Turning the knob one direction engages HPF, the other LPF. — [DJ Class: filters and FX](https://djclass.net/learn/filters-and-fx); [Unison: filter sweeps](https://unison.audio/filter-sweeps/) (production-oriented)
- Build into a drop: slow high-pass sweep over a few bars, then return the filter to center on the drop so bass returns with impact. — [DJ Class: filters and FX](https://djclass.net/learn/filters-and-fx)
- Two-track filter blend: bring incoming track in with low end rolled off, sweep a high-pass up on the outgoing during its last phrase and cut it on the boundary. Forum: filter track one ~4 bars, bring track two in filtered, slowly reduce track 2's filter to 0. — [DJ TechTools forum: which effects work for transitioning](https://forum.djtechtools.com/t/which-effects-work-for-transitioning/63584) (forum)
- On Pioneer filters, past roughly 2-3 o'clock may not sound good (forum opinion). — same forum link.
- Overuse makes the room stop noticing; save for transitions you want heard. — same.
- Echo-out steps: start at end of a 16/32-bar phrase; set a beat-synced delay (1/4 or 1/8, or 1 beat); engage on the last beat you want repeated; pull channel fader down immediately so only the echo continues; bring the incoming track in under the decaying repeats. Mistakes: engaging mid-phrase; leaving the channel fader up. — [We Are Crossfader: three easy steps to mix any genre](https://wearecrossfader.co.uk/blog/how-to-mix-any-genre-in-three-easy-steps/); [Mixgraph: echo out](https://www.mixgraph.io/glossary/echo-out); [Digital DJ Pool: transitions](https://digitaldjpool.com/blog/dj-transitions-for-beginners/)
- Echo-outs are most useful when records have nothing in common, or a track lacks an outro. Feedback behavior varies by device/software: one forum user found raising feedback made the tail louder, not longer. — [DJ TechTools forum: key change + echo freeze](https://forum.djtechtools.com/t/key-change-echo-freeze/47859) (forum)

### Inferences
- Echo-out drill: pick a track with a hard stop at a phrase end; try 1-beat, 1/2-beat and 2-beat echoes and find which fits the tempo; add the incoming track's first kick on the bar after the final repeat.
- Mistake: leaving the echo on long enough that it clashes with the incoming key. (Reasoned, unsourced.)

### Gaps
- Effect names/knob locations differ by software (rekordbox Beat FX Echo, Serato Echo, Traktor Delay T3 etc.); not retrieved.

## 6. Manual beatmatching beyond sync (drift correction, pitch bend, jog nudging)

### Takeaway
After setting tempo, drift is fixed with a short nudge/bend to re-align the beat plus a tiny tempo adjustment to stop it recurring; always adjust only the incoming track, keep corrections small and frequent, and diagnose drift direction by ear.

### Cited Findings
- Workflow: a temporary nudge re-aligns beats; a small pitch fader change locks the tempo (nudge without a tempo change just drifts again); loop: listen 4-8 bars, diagnose, nudge, make a ~0.05% fader tweak, listen again; adjust only the incoming track; a flam between kicks shows which track leads. — [StudyRaid: manual beatmatching by ear](https://app.studyraid.com/en/read/110800/5048862/manual-beatmatching-by-ear) (single instructor, not an industry standard)
- Pitch bend is a momentary phase fix; keep it small and repeated; large bends are audible as drum wobble; repeated nudges in one direction mean the tempo or beatgrid needs fixing. — [Mixgraph: pitch bend](https://www.mixgraph.io/glossary/pitch-bend)
- Nudging the jog wheel is distinct from the tempo slider and pitch-bend buttons; fine fader resolution matters (14-bit) for precise correction. — [Digital DJ Tips: nudge, tempo slider & pitch bend](https://www.digitaldjtips.com/nudge-tempo-slider-pitch-bend/); [Digital DJ Tips: why jogwheels don't alter BPM permanently](https://www.digitaldjtips.com/your-questions-why-dont-jogwheels-alter-bpms-permanently/)
- Forum: coarse pitch faders made matching 140 BPM exactly impossible, causing re-drift. — [DJ TechTools forum: stepping it up](https://forum.djtechtools.com/t/stepping-it-up/13050)

### Inferences
- Steps (standard practice): hear the outgoing beat in speakers and the incoming in headphones; start the incoming track on the beat; if incoming is ahead, nudge back (or if it keeps speeding ahead, lower the tempo a hair); use jog edge or bend buttons; check again after 8 bars.
- Mistakes: touching both decks; big tempo moves rather than micro-moves; trusting the beatgrid on tracks with live drummers (grid may not fit; sync/quantize can mislead); touching the jog top in vinyl mode, which stops audio.
- Drill: mix two tracks with sync and quantize off for 32 bars; log how many nudges needed; aim for fewer than two.

### Gaps
- Specific controller-by-controller jog mode labels not retrieved.

## 7. Tempo transitions across genres (half/double time, ramps, bridge tracks, breakdown transitions)

### Takeaway
You can jump between BPM zones by matching a track to exactly half or double the tempo (70 ↔ 140), by ramping tempo gradually with key lock, or with a cut/effect/breakdown/bridge track. Check "felt" vs "written" tempo before you commit.

### Cited Findings
- Pairing with a track at exactly half or double tempo lines up beats (70/140; 87/174, called the "tempo octave"). One bar of the half-time song equals two bars of the other, so match bar counts across decks (e.g., 4 bars vs 8 bars). — [Digital DJ Tips: half and double time BPMs](https://www.digitaldjtips.com/how-to-mix-using-half-time-and-double-time-bpms/); [dj.studio: DJ tempo change techniques](https://dj.studio/blog/dj-tempo-change-techniques)
- Use for hip-hop to drum and bass/dubstep; half-time switches usually work better as a quick cut than a long crossfade. — same sources.
- Felt tempo differs from labeled tempo: dubstep labeled 140 feels like 70; trap labeled 130-150 can feel half-time; check grid/metadata. — [Digital DJ Pool: DJ BPM guide](https://digitaldjpool.com/blog/dj-bpm-guide/); [DJ Class: mixing between genres](https://djclass.net/learn/mixing-between-genres)
- Non-beatmatched alternatives: drop the new track on its hook, or cut to a beatless break at a different tempo. — [Digital DJ Tips: 7 tempo change tips](https://www.digitaldjtips.com/7-insider-tips-making-exciting-tempo-changes-djing/)
- Gradual ramp: sync the incoming track, then slowly raise its pitch toward its native BPM over successive tracks; others say you can shift most of the difference in one go depending on where you mix in. — [DJ TechTools forum: low to high BPM](https://forum.djtechtools.com/t/best-way-to-beat-match-and-transition-from-low-to-high-bpm-and-vice-versa/55244) (forum)
- Keep key lock on and drag the pitch fader slowly so vocals/melody pitch stays constant. — [VirtualDJ forum: Mixing](https://virtualdj.com/forums/179935/Music_discussion/Mixing.html) (forum)
- Hardware tempo fader may not take effect until it matches the software position (soft takeover, djay). Small increments matter; a fader jumping 1-1.5% is audible. — [Algoriddim community](https://community.algoriddim.com/t/adjusting-tempo-after-transition-to-another-track/23677); [VirtualDJ forum: changing BPM gradually](https://virtualdj.com/forums/146973/General_Discussion/Changing_BPM_gradually.html)
- Big BPM gaps: a loop/echo-freeze trick (sync off, widen tempo range to 100%, slowly move incoming tempo for a rising effect), or use an effect, cut or fade. — [DJ TechTools: BPM transition tag](https://djtechtools.com/amp/tag/bpm-transition/); [VirtualDJ forum](https://virtualdj.com/forums/179935/Music_discussion/Mixing.html)
- Breakdown/drop swap: a drop swap (see section 8) and swapping vocals during breakdowns are cited under breakdown guidance. — [We Are Crossfader: 12 high-energy transitions](https://wearecrossfader.co.uk/blog/12-high-energy-dj-transitions/)

### Inferences
- Bridge track: no dedicated source found; the concept (a track at an intermediate BPM or without a hard beat to connect two zones) is common DJ practice, add labeled "common practice."
- Step recipe for half-time: set cue at start of a phrase in the half-time track; sync tempo to half/double of the other (check software "half/double BPM" button or the beatgrid); count bars at the correct ratio; do a quick EQ swap or cut at the phrase.
- Mistakes: matching to wrong octave (ear vs grid); ramping by large fader jumps; forgetting key lock so the pitch of vocals rises.
- Drill: mix a 75 BPM hip-hop track into a 150 BPM track, and a 128 house track into a 140 techno track via a 4-bar tempo ramp.

### Gaps
- No readable source on "bridge tracks" specifically; no genre pair playlists.

## 8. Looping and cue tricks in transitions

### Takeaway
Loops extend short outros, loop rolls build tension, hot cues launch tracks at phrase-aligned points, and beat jump fixes phrasing; backspins/brakes end tracks that can't be beatmatched. Use sparingly.

### Cited Findings
- Loop on a short outro to hold the outgoing track while the incoming comes in, then exit the loop to finish the swap; hot cues launch the incoming track from a phrase-aligned point; cue juggling between two cues makes quick edits. — [DJ Class: looping and hot cues](https://djclass.net/learn/looping-and-hot-cues)
- Loop roll: used in build-ups/breakdowns; playhead keeps moving underneath, so releasing the pad resumes where the track would have been; use sparingly. — [Alesis: understanding roll mode](https://support.alesis.com/support/solutions/articles/69000858314-understanding-roll-mode); [We Are Crossfader](https://wearecrossfader.co.uk/?p=222258)
- Loop tightening: slowly shorten the loop to build tension before a drop; works best on distinctive sounds (vocal, drum hit, synth). — [We Are Crossfader: how to mix EDM](https://wearecrossfader.co.uk/blog/how-to-mix-edm/)
- Beat jump example: incoming track cued with a longer intro than expected, a 32-beat jump corrects phrasing instead of looping. — [DJ TechTools: underused techniques, beat jump (2016)](https://djtechtools.com/2016/09/15/underused-dj-techniques-beat-jump)
- Beatless intros: set a cue, beatmatch, then beat jump back to complete the transition; depends on accurate beatgrids. — [Digital DJ Tips: beat jump for beatless intros in Serato](https://www.digitaldjtips.com/?p=2752659)
- Serato: beat loops easier than manual loops (manual risk phase drift); saved loops share slots with cue points. — [Bonedo: Loops und Beat Jumps in Serato DJ](https://www.bonedo.de/artikel/loops-und-beat-jumps-in-serato-dj/) (German)
- Drop swap: let the crowd expect track A's drop, play both breakdowns in phrase, then kill A completely when B's drop hits; if breakdowns differ in length the drops won't align. — [We Are Crossfader: 12 high-energy transitions](https://wearecrossfader.co.uk/blog/12-high-energy-dj-transitions/)
- Backspin/spinback: count out a 4-bar phrase, start the backspin on the 3rd beat of the last bar, next track starts on the downbeat; stop the first track after the spin or it restarts; on controllers use cue points (spin back to a drop, hit a hot cue to extend it). Brake length sets feel (long = slow sweep, short = abrupt). A spinback ends a track when beatmatching isn't possible; aim to land on a downbeat. — [zZounds: improve your mix with a well-placed backspin](https://blog.zzounds.com/2020/01/10/improve-your-mix-with-a-well-placed-backspin/); [Mixgraph: spinback](https://www.mixgraph.io/glossary/spinback); [Mixgraph: backspin](https://www.mixgraph.io/glossary/backspin)
- Serato Flip automates a hot-cue sequence saved to the track; useful for intro edits. — [We Are Crossfader: Serato Flip tutorial](https://wearecrossfader.co.uk/blog/serato-flip-tutorial/)

### Inferences
- Steps (standard practice): set 4 hot cues (A = phrase start, B = drop, C = breakdown start, D = outro); beat jump size 16/32 for intro correction; loop roll 1/2 beat into 1/8 on the last bar, release on the downbeat of the next phrase.
- Mistakes: loops not beat-quantized so they smear; looping a section with vocals that repeat awkwardly; overuse of backspins; forgetting to stop the outgoing track.
- Drill: take a track with a 16-bar intro; shorten it to 8 using a beat jump of 8 bars, then extend a 8-bar outro to 16 with a loop; 5 reps each.

### Gaps
- No readable rekordbox-specific instructions (pad modes, "Beat Jump" vs "Slicer") or Traktor Remix Deck notes. Need official manuals.

## 9. Transition planning (what to listen for; mixing in/out of breakdowns)

### Takeaway
Before every transition, check energy, key, vocal/melody density and structure at the overlap zone; put the blend where tracks are sparse and use a short blend or swap when both have vocals or melodies.

### Cited Findings
- Clashes: overlapping melodies clash when keys don't match; in long overlaps both melodies are audible so a mismatch becomes painful; both tracks having vocals creates competing voices; shorten the blend or switch technique when you hear melodies fighting. — [Mixgraph: DJ transition techniques](https://www.mixgraph.io/learn/dj-transition-techniques); [Mixgraph: vocal mixing guide](https://www.mixgraph.io/learn/vocal-mixing-guide)
- Breakdown handling: swap vocals during breakdowns so the outgoing vocal exits as the incoming enters; cutting the incoming mids and then swapping creates a vocal-range crossfade (this overlaps with the already-published midrange vocal separation lesson, so mention at most in one line). Keep that blend short. — [Mixgraph: vocal mixing guide](https://www.mixgraph.io/learn/vocal-mixing-guide)
- Drop timing: if a track comes in too early/late the drops clash; one source ranks tempo as most immediately important. — [We Are Crossfader: how to mix EDM](https://wearecrossfader.co.uk/blog/how-to-mix-edm/)
- Forum thread on transitioning between breakdowns and build-ups (not summarized): [DJ TechTools forum: transition between breakdowns/buildups](https://forum.djtechtools.com/t/transition-between-breakdowns-buildups/42056)
- Energy: pair key with energy level; a compatible key doesn't guarantee fit. — [Mixed In Key](https://mixedinkey.com/workflows/change-energy-with-camelot-wheel/)

### Inferences
- Pre-mix checklist for carousel (reasoned): (1) Energy: up, flat, down? (2) Key: within +/-1 or A/B? (3) Vocals: is anyone singing in the overlap zone? (4) Melody: are both lead melodies active? (5) Structure: where is the next breakdown/drop? (6) Pick technique (long blend, bass swap, cut, echo-out, drop swap).
- Mixing out of a breakdown: bring the incoming drum loop/low-end-free intro under the outgoing breakdown, then let the incoming drop hit as the outgoing build resolves (drop swap).
- Drill: for 10 tracks in your crate, write the first phrase's content (drums only/vocal/melody) and best exit point; then pair the tracks that have a drums-only exit with a drums-only intro.
