## 1. Set construction: energy arcs, slot roles, set length, ordering, storytelling, planning vs improvising, safety crate

### Takeaway
Sources agree on a rough arc of open, build, peak, sustain, close, with the warm-up setting mood rather than energy and the closer easing down. The consensus on planning is hybrid: plan structure and track clusters, bring more music than you will play, and decide the running order live.

### Cited Findings
- A "five-phase arc" is described as open, build, peak, sustain and close, programmed this way rather than as a flat run of high-energy tracks. Rough split: open is the first 15-20%, build runs to about the halfway point, peak runs about 50-80% (energy levels 7-9 on a 1-10 scale, sustained "through tension-release cycles rather than constant climax"), close is the final 10-15%. These are rules of thumb from blog and vendor guides, not formal research. The summary did not say which of djclass.net, Mixgraph or DJ Will Gill gave which figure. — [djclass how to build a dj set](https://djclass.net/learn/how-to-build-a-dj-set); [Mixgraph energy flow guide](https://www.mixgraph.io/learn/energy-flow-guide); [DJ Will Gill, Playlist Curation Tips 2026](https://djwillgill.com/playlist-curation-tips-for-aspiring-djs/); [Mixgraph set planning guide](https://www.mixgraph.io/learn/dj-set-planning-guide)
- Warm-up role: the warm-up DJ should "set tone, not energy". Save your favourite peak-time track for the peak slot, not the opening. — [Dubspot, tips for opening DJs](https://blog.dubspot.com/tips-for-opening-djs-josh-wink-hector-romero-mike-huckaby-dj-dan-shiftee); [DJ Will Gill](https://djwillgill.com/playlist-curation-tips-for-aspiring-djs/)
- Peak role: build toward big moments but leave breathers; "a peak with no valley before it isn't a peak". — [DJ Will Gill, song selection for setlist flow](https://djwillgill.com/pro-tactics-for-flawless-song-selection-for-dj-setlist-flow/) (paraphrase of search summary; exact wording attribution unverified)
- Closing role: ease the room down; end on a satisfying, often more emotional or melodic note instead of the hardest track. — [DJ Will Gill](https://djwillgill.com/playlist-curation-tips-for-aspiring-djs/)
- Ordering tools: hold BPM steady within an energy block and step up between blocks; stay in one key for 2-3 tracks before moving to an adjacent key; rate tracks on a 1-10 energy scale and move one level at a time. — [Mixgraph energy flow guide](https://www.mixgraph.io/learn/energy-flow-guide); [djclass](https://djclass.net/learn/how-to-build-a-dj-set)
- Not every set needs a full arc: deep house warm-ups and ambient sets often stay in a narrow energy band. — [Mixgraph set planning guide](https://www.mixgraph.io/learn/dj-set-planning-guide)
- Set length and crate size: a SetFlow guide says a 2-hour set is roughly 34 tracks, and to pack 51-68 so you can read the room. Its core rule is "a roadmap, not a script". The vendor sells a set-planning tool, so this may be promotional. — [SetFlow, how to plan a DJ set](https://www.setflow.app/blog/how-to-plan-a-dj-set)
- Hybrid approach: effective DJs blend preparation with flexibility. Preparation gives a safety net and "a toolbox of reliable combinations". — [DJ Will Gill, pre-planned vs improvised](https://djwillgill.com/pre-planned-vs-improvised-dj-curated-sets/)
- Plan "clusters and pathways", not a minute-by-minute tracklist: groups of tracks that work together by energy and key. — [DJ Will Gill, pre-planned vs improvised](https://djwillgill.com/pre-planned-vs-improvised-dj-curated-sets/); [SetFlow](https://www.setflow.app/blog/how-to-plan-a-dj-set)
- Storytelling: organise the library in a chapter-based structure, so while performing you ask what the next chapter or energy shift should be. — [djoid, build a set that tells a story](https://www.djoid.io/articles/how-to-build-a-dj-set-that-tells-a-story-not-just-one-that-works)
- Crates: group tracks into sub-crates for quick access. — [Roland Resource Centre, how to craft a good DJ set](https://rolandcorp.com.au/blog/how-to-craft-a-good-dj-set)
- A rigid script makes it hard to change direction if things go wrong (one DJ's blog account). Context matters: a high-stakes corporate awards show suits a pre-planned set, while a late-night festival slot rewards improvisation. — [DJ Will Gill, pre-planned vs improvised](https://djwillgill.com/pre-planned-vs-improvised-dj-curated-sets/); [Substack, The Playlist Mistake](https://reallychrism.substack.com/p/the-playlist-mistake-killing-your)
- Library organisation by energy, BPM, era or key lets you choose fast when the room shifts. — [Digital DJ Tips, how to read a crowd](https://www.digitaldjtips.com/how-to-read-a-crowd-and-what-happens-if-you-dont/)
- Safety track fallback: a USB stick with 20-40 songs ready to go at all times. — [DJ TechTools, 9 tips from the community 2021](https://djtechtools.com/2021/09/29/preparing-for-any-dj-gig-9-tips-from-the-djtt-community) (summary attributes it to this result set; exact page unverified)

### Inferences
- Carousel steps: (1) Ask the slot role and total length. (2) Draw the arc as percentages of time. (3) Tag each track 1-10 energy plus key. (4) Build 3-4 clusters per energy block. (5) Add 1.5x to 2x the tracks you need. (6) Pick the order live, one energy level at a time.
- Common mistakes: opening with your biggest track; flat energy all night; a rigid minute-by-minute list; ending on the hardest track; no safety crate.
- Drill: take a 60-minute slot. Write the percentage breakdown (for example 9 min open, 21 min build, 18 min peak, 6 min close from the 15-20 / 50 / 50-80 / 10-15 splits, noting the sources overlap loosely). Pick 25 tracks, score energy, then build three different 10-track routes through them.
- The percentages come from unattributed rule-of-thumb sources, so present them as "a common guideline", not fact.

### Gaps
- No curriculum-level source (Point Blank, Dubspot course pages) on set-length planning was retrievable.
- No source gave a tested, evidence-based ratio of planned to improvised tracks.
- Warm-up-specific BPM guidance (for example starting 10 BPM below the headliner) was not found.

## 2. Reading and managing the crowd: signals, requests, dead floors

### Takeaway
Reading the room means watching the floor (not your gear), testing with variety, and treating repeat requests as data. Requests should be collected in a system, played at a fitting moment, and declined politely with a redirect.

### Cited Findings
- Common beginner error: staring at equipment all set; strong DJs read and react to the audience. — [DJ TechTools, reading the dance floor](https://djtechtools.com/2018/01/08/reading-dance-floor-important-dj-skill/)
- Do not assume a quiet floor means failure: some guests do not want to dance or feel self-conscious. A crowd is a collective but its people are individuals. — [Digital DJ Tips, how to read a crowd](https://www.digitaldjtips.com/how-to-read-a-crowd-and-what-happens-if-you-dont/) (2011 article)
- Do not simply give the crowd what it asks for; repeated requests for a style are information for shaping a set that still reflects your taste. — [Digital DJ Tips](https://www.digitaldjtips.com/how-to-read-a-crowd-and-what-happens-if-you-dont/)
- Research client favourites and least-favourites ahead of time. — [Digital DJ Tips, reading the crowd at weddings](https://digitaldjtips.com/?p=2567261)
- Test the room with a wide mix of styles early, then build from the response (forum report). If you are not playing the whole night, watch the previous DJ's crowd. — [DJ TechTools forum, how do you read a crowd](https://forum.djtechtools.com/t/how-do-you-read-a-crowd-for-dummies/71224)
- Skill is partly experience: a forum contributor says it "takes a long time to master"; Electronic Beats calls it "more of a mysterious art than a hard science" but offers pointers. — [DJ TechTools forum](https://forum.djtechtools.com/t/how-do-you-read-a-crowd-for-dummies/71224); [Electronic Beats](https://electronicbeats.net/the-feed/read-dance-floor-like-favorite-underground-dj)
- Dead-floor recovery (indirect): if the crowd is not responding, check whether you are playing what you think they should like rather than what they respond to in the moment. Flow from track to track is needed for people to get into a groove; return to planned sets that have worked before. Choose the next track in the same or a compatible key. One DJ found success playing something they enjoy but had not planned. This came from a search that found no dedicated dead-floor guide. — [DJ TechTools, recover from your worst DJ fails (2019)](https://djtechtools.com/2019/11/18/when-things-go-wrong-how-to-recover-from-your-worst-dj-fails/)
- Request collection: clipboard or notepad ("pen and paper" called a top tip), text-in services, or QR-code request apps (Lime DJ, with a reorderable queue and no app download). The Lime DJ page is the vendor's own. — [Digital DJ Tips, handle requests](https://www.digitaldjtips.com/how-to-handle-requests-when-djing/); [The DJ Revolution](https://www.thedjrevolution.com/how-to-manage-song-requests/); [Lime DJ](https://limedj.com/)
- Playing requests: wait for a natural spot; do not abandon a planned transition because a request arrived; keep a separate request playlist. — [DJKit, how DJs handle requests](https://www.djkit.com/blogs/news/how-djs-handle-song-requests); [Digital DJ Tips](https://www.digitaldjtips.com/how-to-handle-requests-when-djing/)
- Saying no: scripts such as "Love that one, I'll find a spot for it later, can't promise exactly when" and "Great song, but it doesn't quite fit the vibe I've got going right now". If you do not own it, say so politely. Acknowledge, redirect, and do not let one person hijack your focus from the room. Confirm with the client beforehand whether guests may request at all. — [DJ City, annoying requests](https://djcity.com.au/blog/how-to-handle-annoying-song-requests-as-a-dj/); [The DJ Revolution](https://www.thedjrevolution.com/how-to-manage-song-requests/)
- Streaming as a request fallback: test at home first; use a phone hotspot if the venue has no Wi-Fi. A broad, well-organised library is what lets you say yes. — [DJKit](https://www.djkit.com/blogs/news/how-djs-handle-song-requests) (summary-level attribution)
- Mobile DJs get more requests than club DJs because events are built around guest experience, not the DJ's musical identity. — [Pioneer DJ blog, guide to mobile DJing](https://blog.pioneerdj.com/djtips/a-djs-guide-to-mobile-djing/) (summary-level attribution)

### Inferences
- Carousel steps for a read: (1) Watch the floor before each transition. (2) Note who is on the floor (age, group size, are they staying through the whole track?). (3) Test a different genre or era for 1-2 tracks. (4) Keep what holds the floor; drop what clears it. (5) Log repeat requests as the style signal.
- Dead-floor sequence: check whether the issue is the music or the moment (dinner, speeches, timing); go to familiar, groove-friendly tracks at a similar tempo; avoid dramatic genre jumps; then step up energy one level at a time. This ordering is my synthesis, not a sourced procedure.
- Common mistakes: head-down mixing, obeying every request, rejecting rudely, abandoning a transition for a request.
- Drill: "Read-and-log": run a 20-minute practice set (video of a real room if no gig) and write every 5 minutes: floor count, mood, what changed. Practise delivering the "save it for later" line.

### Gaps
- No source gave concrete observable signals (for example "people turning toward the DJ", "dancers leaving after the drop") in the retrieved summaries. London Sound Academy "How DJs Read The Crowd" (https://www.londonsoundacademy.com/blog/how-djs-read-the-crowd) appeared in results but its content was not retrieved.
- No dedicated dead-floor recovery guide was found.
- No Reddit threads retrieved.

## 3. Genre blending and BPM ranges

### Takeaway
Ranges are loose and sources disagree on edges. Bridging uses overlapping tempo bands (90-110 for hip-hop to house; pop at 100-125 as a connector), half-time and double-time relationships, and vocal or instrumental bridges. Tempo match alone is not enough; key and groove matter too.

### Cited Findings
- Reported ranges (sources disagree): House 120-130 in one guide, 124-130 for house, tech house and bass house in another; deep and afro house about 118-124. Techno about 125-150 in one chart and 130-140 in another; hard techno's typical floor has moved to 145-160 since 2021. Hip-hop estimates run from 70-90 to 85-95 (iZotope) and 90-110 (Native Instruments); trap is often felt at half-time, around 130-160. Afrobeats about 105-115, with amapiano at a similar 105-115. Check chart dates. — [Digital DJ Pool, DJ BPM Guide (2026)](https://digitaldjpool.com/blog/dj-bpm-guide/); [Mixgraph BPM guide](https://www.mixgraph.io/learn/bpm-guide); [Orphiq BPM chart](https://orphiq.com/resources/bpm-tempo-guide); [Sample Focus](https://blog.samplefocus.com/blog/ultimate-guide-to-tempo-and-bpm-the-best-bpms-for-hip-hop-trap-dnb-and-more) (which figure came from which page was not preserved in the summary)
- The 90-110 band (reggaeton, dancehall, some pop) is described as the gap between hip-hop and house. UK garage at 130-138 is a bridge between house and drum and bass. — [Digital DJ Pool](https://digitaldjpool.com/blog/dj-bpm-guide/) and the Mixgraph guide (summary-level attribution)
- Half-time/double-time: 70 with 140 and 87 with 174 keep beats aligned. A careful guide gives hip-hop/R&B about 70-100, house at double-time (85 is about 170; 64 is about 128), and pop about 100-125 as "the universal bridge". — [PulseDJ open format guide](https://blog.pulsedj.com/open-format-djing) and the Mixgraph guides (summary-level attribution)
- Source error to avoid: one source claims hip-hop at 90 doubles cleanly to house at 128; the search summary flagged that 90 doubled is 180, not 128. Do not repeat that claim. A valid hip-hop-to-house route is pitching up to the overlap band or using a vocal or instrumental bridge. — [dj.studio open format guide](https://dj.studio/blog/open-format-djing) (the page the claim came from was not confirmed)
- Open-format transition techniques: find an overlapping BPM range and use it as an on-ramp; bring the outgoing vocal up alone, drop its instrumental, and start the new track underneath so the vocal carries the listener across; use a short instrumental or acapella bridge when keys clash; wordplay links via a shared lyric or phrase; build a bridge library of transitional tracks (long intros, minimal vocals); tag tracks by genre, BPM, key and energy; plan genre pivots in advance. — [Slappers n Bangers, 7 core transition techniques](https://slappersnbangers.com/open-format-dj/); [Mixed In Key open-format library](https://mixedinkey.com/workflows/prepare-library-for-open-format-djing/); [NaJade](https://djnajade.com/open-format-djing/); [PulseDJ](https://blog.pulsedj.com/open-format-djing)
- Matching tempo is "necessary but not sufficient"; key and groove also matter. — [Mixgraph](https://www.mixgraph.io/learn/bpm-guide)

### Inferences
- The afro house to Afrobeats connection (about 118-124 vs about 105-115) is my inference from ranges, not a stated source claim.
- Carousel idea: a "BPM map" graphic showing genre bands, with arrows for x2 and x0.5 jumps and the 90-110 and 100-125 bridge zones highlighted. Must label ranges as "approximate, sources vary".
- Steps: (1) Know each track's true BPM and whether it is felt at half-time. (2) Find the shared band or the doubling. (3) Choose a bridge track (pop, garage, afro house). (4) Use a vocal carry or a loop-and-pitch handoff. (5) Confirm key.
- Common mistakes: trusting a chart over your ears; jumping genres without a bridge; mixing at half-time confusion (counting 70 as 70 vs 140); forgetting to tag bridge tracks.
- Drill: pick a hip-hop track at about 90 and a house track at about 124. Find three different bridges (pop, dancehall or reggaeton, half-time/double-time) and record each transition.

### Gaps
- No source on Latin (salsa, bachata, cumbia) BPM ranges was returned, and reggaeton was only cited as 90-110. EDM and pop-chart BPM specifics are also thin.
- No source on how to handle tempo drift in older or live-recorded tracks.

## 4. Vocals, acapellas, instrumentals, mashups on the fly, tempo-syncing across genres

(Overlaps the already published midrange-vocal and EQ topics; focus here is the live use and the matching workflow only.)

### Takeaway
Check tempo first, then key, then phrase alignment by ear. Keep the BPM gap small, align phrases to the grid, and match sections. Live mashups are often an acapella layered over a break via hot cues.

### Cited Findings
- Keep tracks within 5-10 BPM to avoid artefacts; time-stretch small changes only; vocals sound most natural at their original BPM; for far-apart tempos pick an intermediate tempo (for example 100 and 128). — [dj.studio, acapellas for DJs](https://dj.studio/blog/acapellas-for-djs); [We Are Crossfader, mashup tutorial](https://wearecrossfader.co.uk/blog/dj-mashup-tutorial/)
- Key: harmonic-mixing tools (Mixed In Key, rekordbox) flag compatible keys; related Camelot keys work; do not pitch-shift more than a couple of semitones; the true test is the ear against the strongest chorus notes. — [Wikipedia, harmonic mixing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harmonic_mixing); [The Vocal Market](https://thevocalmarket.com/blogs/how-to/how-to-use-acapellas-in-your-music-production-5-methods)
- Alignment: align phrases to the grid, not just the tempo; set a cue on the first strong syllable; match verse to verse and chorus to chorus. For live work, layer an acapella over a break using hot cues or sample triggers. — [dj.studio](https://dj.studio/blog/acapellas-for-djs); [We Are Crossfader](https://wearecrossfader.co.uk/blog/dj-mashup-tutorial/)
- Mix cleanup: high-pass the acapella's low end; make space in the instrumental around the vocal band (roughly 200 Hz-4 kHz); light compression, light reverb and delay. (This overlaps the published EQ and midrange topics, so likely skip.) — [We Are Crossfader](https://wearecrossfader.co.uk/blog/dj-mashup-tutorial/)
- Stems make vocal-carry transitions possible with almost any track. — [Mixgraph glossary and dj.studio via open-format result set](https://www.mixgraph.io/glossary/open-format) (summary-level)

### Inferences
- Carousel: "3-check mashup" (tempo, key, phrase). Drill: layer one acapella over three different instrumentals within 8 BPM, noting which one clashes and why.
- Most retrieved material is production-oriented (Ableton, DAW), so live-DJ specifics are my adaptation.

### Gaps
- Little source material on live mashups specifically on controllers (stems on Serato/rekordbox/Traktor, hot-cue acapella triggers).
- No source on legality or licensing of acapellas in live sets.

## 5. Performance habits: pre-gig checklist, soundcheck, redundancy, mistakes live, stage presence, hearing and hydration

### Takeaway
Arrive early, introduce yourself to the engineer, carry duplicates of anything that can fail (headphones, USB, cables), and have a plan to keep sound going while you fix a failure. Hearing protection means high-fidelity earplugs and lower monitors.

### Cited Findings
- Checklist items that recur: second pair of headphones and a 1/8" to 1/4" adapter; one or two extra USB drives, plugged in beforehand to confirm tracks load; a fallback USB of 20-40 songs; export a dated settings file before shutdown; spare cables, power strips and extension cords, gaffer tape, flashlight. — [Digital DJ Tips, ultimate checklist](https://www.digitaldjtips.com/the-ultimate-checklist-of-things-to-take-to-your-dj-gigs/); [DJ TechTools, 9 tips (2021)](https://djtechtools.com/2021/09/29/preparing-for-any-dj-gig-9-tips-from-the-djtt-community); [MusicianWave 50 items](https://www.musicianwave.com/djs-gig-checklist-50-essential-items/)
- Venue contact in advance on equipment you bring vs what is on site; note how monitoring differs. — [DJ TechTools, critical steps before every digital DJ gig (2013)](https://djtechtools.com/2013/12/08/critical-steps-before-every-digital-dj-gig); [MusicRadar, 7 ways to play better gigs](https://www.musicradar.com/how-to/7-ways-to-play-better-gigs)
- On arrival: introduce yourself to the sound engineer, say what gear you have and what you need from the monitors ("they can't help you if they don't know"); arrive early to inspect the booth, test outlets, check stability; check volume levels for hearing and audience comfort. — [MusicRadar](https://www.musicradar.com/how-to/7-ways-to-play-better-gigs); [DJ TechTools 2013](https://djtechtools.com/2013/12/08/critical-steps-before-every-digital-dj-gig)
- Equipment failure: keep one source playing while you fix the other; loop or extend the playing track to buy time; reboot the dead unit; stay composed because the crowd takes cues from you. — [EDM Ghost Production, recovering from equipment failure](https://edm-ghost-production.com/dj-knowledge-base/recovering-from-equipment-failure)
- Train-wreck recovery: re-cue and retry if time remains on the outgoing track; use talk-over to duck the mess plus a brief apology; hide with echo, delay, reverb or filters but reset them before the next track; pick the stronger track, pull the other down over a bar, and prefer a clean exit over a heroic rescue; if drift worsens after two nudges, exit. Prevention: check the downbeat, count phrases in 8, 16 and 32 bars, keep one track dominant in the low end. Forums say the crowd often does not notice, so do not draw attention. — [Digital DJ Tips, 5 ways to salvage a trainwreck](https://www.digitaldjtips.com/5-tricks-to-recover-from-a-trainwreck/); [Ghost Production, trainwreck mixes](https://theghostproduction.com/trainwreck-mixes-fixed-dj-set/); [Dubstepforum thread](https://www.dubstepforum.com/forum/viewtopic.php?t=156102&start=20)
- Stage presence: body language leads the crowd; standing still drains the room; start small with eye contact across sections, gestures, walking closer; do not hide behind the laptop; acknowledge reactions with a nod or raised hand. — [London Sound Academy, stage presence](https://www.londonsoundacademy.com/blog/elevating-your-dj-game-mastering-stage-presence); [4D4M](https://4d4m.com/the-importance-of-stage-presence-for-edm-djs-and-how-to-improve-it/); [oboe.com intro to DJing](https://oboe.com/learn/introduction-to-djing-q32srr/performance-and-stage-presence-4)
- Booth etiquette: do not interrupt a working DJ; keep the booth clear; ask before offering drinks. — [Festival101](https://festival101.co.za/how-to-approach-a-dj-at-a-club-the-ultimate-dj-booth-etiquette-guide/); [EDM Ghost, booth and club etiquette](https://edm-ghost-production.com/dj-knowledge-base/booth-and-club-etiquette)
- Hearing: filtered "high-fidelity" earplugs reduce volume evenly across frequencies; Etymotic ER20/ER20XS about 20 USD as entry level; custom-moulded 200-500 USD; generic foam plugs muffle highs. Booth monitors are a major exposure source and are often too loud; lower them between mixes; take 2-3 minute breaks; set monitor position and EQ at soundcheck instead of raising volume. One source states the WHO-model weekly limit is reached after 20 minutes at 100 dB; avoid frequent long sets. — [Loop Earplugs for DJs](https://us.loopearplugs.com/pages/earplugs-for-djs) (vendor); [Digital DJ Tips, 7 ways to protect hearing](https://www.digitaldjtips.com/7-ways-djs-can-protect-their-hearing/); [DJ TechTools, protect your hearing (2015)](https://djtechtools.com/2015/01/25/what-you-need-to-know-about-protect-your-hearing/); [Recordcase](https://www.recordcase.de/en/hearing-protection-for-djs-levels-exposure-time-headphone-volume-and-warning-signs); [Shush Earplugs](https://shush-earplugs.com/knowledgebase/how-do-djs-protect-their-hearing-during-long-sets/) (vendor)

### Inferences
- Carousel structure for the checklist: Gear, Music, Venue, Body (earplugs, water). A "30-minute early" arrival routine is a plausible teaching hook but no source gave 30 minutes.
- Drill: the "fail on purpose" drill: during practice, unplug one deck, headphones off, loop the live deck for 16 bars, restart; time how long you keep sound uninterrupted. Also record a deliberate train-wreck and practise the clean exit within two nudges.

### Gaps
- Hydration: no retrieved source addressed it; do not claim sourced hydration advice.
- No source on soundcheck specifics beyond engineer introduction and monitor setup (for example gain staging, headroom, limiter) was retrieved.
- Redundancy beyond headphones, USBs and cables (second controller, laptop backup) was only touched indirectly.

## 6. Event-specific intermediate skills: wedding and corporate MC basics, announcements, timelines (DJ craft only)

### Takeaway
Weddings run on cues, not clocks: write the timeline with triggers, pronounce names correctly, keep announcements short, and use music cues to reset the room between formalities.

### Cited Findings
- Hold a pre-event consultation for must-plays and a do-not-play list. — [One of a Kind Event, tips for wedding DJs](https://www.myoneofakindevent.com/tips-for-wedding-djs/)
- Confirm the pronunciation of every name to be announced, including parents and bridal party. — [Photobiz, five things to ask your wedding DJ](https://alewis4.photobiz.com/blog/five-things-to-ask-your-wedding-dj)
- Cue sheets should specify triggers, not just times, because weddings rarely move by the minute (photos run long, dinner service shifts); entries pair a time with a cue source so the MC knows whether to act at a set time or wait for a vendor signal. — [Felix and Fingers, wedding MC cue sheet](https://www.felixandfingers.com/blog/wedding-resources/how-to-create-a-wedding-mc-cue-sheet-your-entertainment-team-can-actually-use/)
- MC delivery: the biggest MC mistake is rushing introductions; pause and build energy before names. Speak clearly, keep announcements short. — [One of a Kind Event, tips to MC a wedding](https://www.myoneofakindevent.com/blog/tips-to-mc-a-wedding/)
- Coordinate with photographer and venue so the first dance and toasts start when everyone is ready; do not talk over the start of someone's favourite song. — [Violet Recording, how to DJ a wedding](https://violetrecording.com/how-to-dj-a-wedding/)
- Dinner music: light, mid-tempo, familiar, nothing too slow or emotional; one source gives a 60-68 dB maximum so guests can converse (single-source figure). — [MacMillan Entertainment, reception DJ timeline (2026)](https://www.macmillangroup.com/blog/2026/07/19/wedding-reception-dj-timeline/); [urequest, wedding music timeline](https://www.urequest.live/blog/wedding-music-timeline/) (summary did not say which page gave 60-68 dB)
- First dance placement varies: before dinner, or after speeches. A DJ forum post suggests placing it right before dancing begins so guests are already watching; ending the first dance with an invitation to the rest of the guests is another approach. Play something right after the entrance to avoid a lull. Short music cues between toasts reset the room. — [Serato forum, DJing a big wedding for the first time](https://serato.com/forum/discussion/1671496); [One of a Kind Event, wedding DJ timeline](https://www.myoneofakindevent.com/blog/ultimate-wedding-dj-timeline/); [DJ Shark Attack timeline](https://www.djsharkattack.com/post/the-ultimate-wedding-reception-timeline-dj-approved)

## 7. Business-of-DJing (brief; only topics seen in school or educator content)

### Takeaway
Educator and forum content favours relationships, opening slots and reliability over cold-emailed mixes; the promo mix is a supporting tool.

### Cited Findings
- Promo mixes run about 30-45 minutes for booking submissions; for bars, pitch a slow weeknight or happy hour and keep the mix under 20 minutes. — [Orphiq, DJ marketing and booking](https://orphiq.com/resources/dj-marketing-booking); [GreenHitz, first residency at a local bar](https://greenhitz.com/blog/how-to-get-dj-residency-local-bar)
- Club owners and promoters rarely have time to listen to promos; offer to open for another DJ or get a friend to recommend you. — [DJ TechTools, how to get a DJ residency (2015)](https://djtechtools.com/amp/2015/04/16/how-to-get-a-dj-residency/)
- Attend regularly so promoters recognise you, then send a short pitch (who you are, what you play, mix link). Starting your own night led to three residencies for one interviewee. Reliability matters; never cancel last minute. — [DJing Tips, start spinning in clubs](https://www.djingtips.com/how-to-dj/how-to-start-spinning-in-clubs/); [On The Rise Academy](http://ontheriseacademy.com/how-to-become-a-resident-dj/); [Your Ghost Production](https://yourghostproduction.com/content/how-to-get-dj-residency)
